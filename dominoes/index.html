Mexican Train

The double-twelve train game. Everyone builds off a shared hub, down their own train and the open Mexican one. Play a double and it has to be answered before anyone moves on. Shed your tiles — low pips win.

How to play

A double-twelve set (91 tiles). Each round opens on a centre double — 12-12 the first round, then 11-11, 10-10, and so on. From the hub run the trains: one for each player, plus one shared Mexican train.

Your turn

  • Add one tile whose end matches the open end of a train you’re allowed to use.
  • You may always build on your own train and the Mexican train. You may build on someone else’s train only while it’s open (a marker is on it).
  • Can’t play? Draw one. If it still won’t go, your turn passes and a marker drops on your train — now anyone can build on it until you play on it yourself.

Doubles must be answered

Play a double and its matching end has to be covered before anyone moves on. If you can cover your own double the same turn, you must. If you can’t, the next player — and the next — has to cover it before doing anything else.

Scoring

The round ends when someone empties their hand (or everyone’s stuck). Everyone else counts the pips still in their hand — those are points, and low is good. Lowest total after the last round wins.

Teaching mode

Trains you can play on are outlined in green, a red outline shows a double waiting to be covered, Hint shows the tile to play and why, and the recap explains each round.
