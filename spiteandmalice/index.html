How to play

Goal: be the first to empty your face-up goal pile (bottom-left). The card on top of your goal pile is your most important card — play it whenever you can.

The centre

There are four shared centre stacks. In the standard game each is built upward from Ace to King: a stack starts with an Ace (or a wild), then 2, 3, 4 … up to King.

A stack is scooped up and recycled into the stock once it holds 13 cards — that pile is the only thing that refills the stock, so filling stacks is what keeps the game moving.

Which way stacks build

Set on the deal screen, and it applies to the whole game:

  • A → K — the standard game. Stacks start at an Ace and climb to King.
  • K → A — the mirror. Stacks start at a King and descend to Ace.
  • Either way — a stack opens with an Ace or a King, and from then on every card may go one up or one down, wrapping King→Ace and Ace→King. Nothing ever reaches a dead end, so the 13-card rule is what scoops a stack. Here the top card stays put — only the 12 beneath it go to the shuffler — so the stack carries straight on from wherever it landed. Far more of your cards will play; so will the bot's.

Your turn

  • You start each turn with 5 cards in hand.
  • Play cards to the centre from three places: the top of your goal pile, your hand, or the top of any side stack.
  • Empty your hand by playing? You immediately draw back up to 5 and keep going.
  • End your turn by moving one hand card to a side stack (your four discard piles). You can play to the centre or discard by tapping.

Wilds

Jokers are wild — play one on any stack and it becomes whatever value that stack needs (or an Ace to start a stack). Save them for the cards you can’t place. In Either way a stack usually needs one of two values, so you choose which one the Joker becomes.

Strategy

  • The centre is shared — advancing a stack can help the bot’s goal card too, so watch its top goal card before you play.
  • Organise your side stacks in descending order (low cards on top) so you can dig them back out.
  • In Either way you can no longer read how close a stack is from its top card — watch the card count instead. It turns red at 11, two from scooping. A scoop drops it to 1, not 0.
